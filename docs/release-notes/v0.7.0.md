# Murmurwater v0.7.0 — Saltbone Reach

The first slice of phase 3: a third water. Grey sea with a long swell on it, slate stacks, and the ribs of something standing out of the water in a double row. Its storms keep hours.

## What's new
- **The whale-road.** Once the Puffin is afloat, Pell remembers another road, north-east of Hearthbay and lit like the old one. The swell on it is more than the skiff will take: she is turned back and told why, and the chart will not send her either. The Puffin takes it, and half way along the waters are Saltbone Reach.
- **Saltbone Reach.** Tryworks Landing (a stone quay, a house under an upturned whaleboat, cold try-pots, a storm mast), the Ribs (nine pairs of them over a channel a boat can sail up, with a brass plate on the ninth), the Jaw, the Anvil, the Kettles (where the swell booms in three holes in a shingle bank), the Long Swell and the Tally Stone. No trees: sea-thrift on the slate, and bones on the beaches.
- **Storms keep hours.** The Reach has three storms and they come at the same hours every day: **Old Margit** at first light (wind and rain), **the Deacon** from two in the afternoon to half past six (the long one, with lightning, and an **eye** from half past three to five in which the sea lies down and gold light comes through), and **Little Wren** from half past ten at night to one (dry lightning). A day is 24 minutes of play. In a storm the swell stands up, the light goes slate and rain comes across; the Lantern Pots take nothing and the hands go below until the eye or the end of it. Your own line is never stopped.
- **Struck water.** While there is lightning, a bolt goes into the sea within a cast or two of you every ten to eighteen seconds and leaves a ring of pale blue fire on the water for half a minute. Cast into it: the bite comes in a few seconds, and what comes up is much more likely to be rare. The first fish hooked there puts the ring out. This is the reason to be out in the Deacon and not tied up waiting for him.
- **Captain Brannagh Oake** on the quay, a whaler who has not put a boat in the water for forty years, with six jobs: eight fish for her almanac, the plate on the ninth rib, a Squall Mackerel, five fish from struck water, a Thunderback off the Anvil while there is lightning, and the count at the Tally Stone.
- **The almanac.** After Oake's first job the screen names the storm that is on and says how long until the next (or until the eye), and the chart has a page for the Reach with the three storms and their hours. A cone goes up the mast on the quay when a storm is on or due within the hour.
- **Ten new fish.** Three common, the Kettle Cod, the Ribfish under the bones, a Squall Mackerel that bites only in a storm, a Lantern Ling in the Jaw by night, the Thunderback off the Anvil while there is lightning, Old Greyback in the Long Swell around noon, and the Lull, which is only there in the eye of the Deacon. They are worth four to five times their rank in the Fen.
- **Gear.** The Whalebone Rod, two baits (Lugworm and Sand Eel), the Petrel Feather (a lure that works in a storm), Oake's sea boots, a pilot coat for filling the journal's Saltbone page, and in the first sea chest fished up there St. Elmo's Bead, which brings the lightning down nearer and oftener.
- **Things fished up there.** A salt crate, sea chests over the Anvil and the Kettles, and four more relics with their rumours.
- **Four tester's starts.** F8 on the title screen now steps through four: this slice's first.

Saves from v0.6 carry over unchanged (the save is still version 6): the weather is worked out from the hour and nothing else, so nothing new is saved and loading a game cannot change it.

## How to run
Download `Murmurwater-0.7.0-portable.exe` and double-click it (Windows may warn that the app is unsigned: choose "More info", then "Run anyway"), or run `Murmurwater-0.7.0-setup.exe` to install it.

## To see this slice without playing up to it
Press **F8** on the title screen. The game comes back as a keeper the simulator played for three and a half hours: late morning on the home dock, the Puffin afloat with both hands aboard, and Pell's whale-road job in hand. Board the Puffin (E, from the dock) and sail north-east: the goal marker and the chart (M) show the road, which begins between two lights about 600 metres out. Keep between the lights. You come out in the Reach at about two in the afternoon, as the Deacon comes up, which is the best way to meet him. Tryworks Landing is straight ahead: tie up at the quay, find Captain Oake, and fish her eight fish off the end of the quay or from the boat in the storm, casting at the rings the lightning leaves. Then the almanac is on the screen. The eye comes over about a minute and a half after the storm begins and lasts about as long. Press F8 again for the older starts, and once more for your own game. Nothing done in a tester's start is saved.

## Known issues
- Captain Oake is a stand-in built in code until her model arrives. The Tally Stone's marks read as a grid and the Lantern Ling's lamp is a speck in the hand: both are being made again.
- The storm sounds (rain, wind, thunder, the struck water's ring) are synthesised stand-ins until the recorded ones arrive. The Reach has no music of its own and Oake has no voice: both wait on credits, like the 121 lines still unvoiced elsewhere.
- Nobody has sailed the Reach's swell or cast at a ring by hand. Whether a ring is easy enough to hit, whether ten to eighteen seconds between bolts is too many or too few, and whether a storm is a pleasure or a nuisance the fifth time are the things most worth an opinion.
- Timings are the simulator's: the Reach is reached at about three hours forty for a practised hand, Oake's six jobs take about an hour and a half more, and the Whalebone Rod is in reach an hour or so after arriving.
- Everything listed under v0.6.0 that is not mentioned here still stands.
