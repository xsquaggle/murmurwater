Fish are normalized to 1 m nose to tail along +Z, so the game scales each to its species length. Glow maps are an `emissiveTexture` on the same material with `KHR_materials_emissive_strength` 1.5, derived from the warm bright texels of the base colour; `docs/art/sheets/emissive-dark.webp` shows them with the lights off.

The ten fish of Mossglass Fen (`reedling` to `the-listener` in the table) are original work built and painted in code, with no generation job: `docs/art/pipeline/build-fen-fish.mjs` holds the ten, `fish.mjs` builds them and `fishmats.py` paints them (`make-fish.sh <fish>` runs it all). Each is 1 m from nose (+Z) to tail with the middle of its bounds at the origin, as the fish above, except that the Sedge Pike and the Stilt Gar have the middle of the body there in height, so that the sprig and the stilts do not move the fish off the point it is held by. One material, `main`, single-sided; 1024 x 512 px base colour (the eel 1024 x 256). The body is lofted through sections from the lips to the root of the tail and both sides share one painted skin; eyes are domes; fins are thin two-faced wedges. The four that glow have an `emissiveTexture` of their own (512 x 256 px, black where nothing glows) and an emissive factor of 1 with no strength extension: `docs/art/sheets/fen-fish-dark.webp` shows them with the lights off (Glowbelly, Old Mossglass, the Listener, the Bottomless Eel).

- Reedling: pencil-thin, straw green with ten dark upright bars, the lower jaw reaching forward and up.
- Mudlark: squat, with an open smiling mouth (modelled, red inside), a cream throat, two pairs of short whiskers.
- Glassfin: leaf-shaped and painted as if clear: the backbone and ribs, a pink sac and a silver bladder show through; cyan edges to every fin. Roughness 0.45.
- Sedge Pike: long and flat-snouted with bark furrows and pale flecks, eyes high on the head; four blades of sedge and a seed head stand on its back.
- Mossback Tench: round-backed with fine scales and red eyes; a cushion of moss sits over the shoulders with two white flowers, one on either side.
- Glowbelly: plump and dark teal; the belly is a lantern of cyan glass with ribs running from end to end, and it is the part that glows.
- Stilt Gar: a needle snout a quarter of its length; four thin fins hang 0.31 to 0.33 m below the body and bow outward.
- Bottomless Eel: ink black at the head going to deep green at the tail, a row of small green pores, pale eyes with pin pupils; a fin runs along the back and the belly and stops short of the tail, which thins to a thread.
- Old Mossglass: a deep carp of bottle green with large gold-rimmed scales, gold whiskers and gold fin rays, an open mouth, and a dark shape painted inside the body. Roughness 0.35.
- The Listener: pale, smooth and eyeless, with a row of dark pores along its side and, in place of pectoral fins, two round horns 0.31 m across on short stems, violet inside.

Notes: the fish stall is the second run of its job (the first, `c941c034-4adf-48ff-b2f8-9cec702029ba`, came back with a blank grey texture and was discarded); its bounds were fitted to 2.6 x 2.4 x 1.45 m with a mild non-uniform scale (`--fit`), about 15 % shallower than generated. The sketchbook has a 512 px texture and was fitted to 0.24 x 0.25 x 0.055 m (thinner than generated) so it sits between Wick's hands. Biscuit is two sculpts, standing and sitting, because the two poses do not deform into each other cleanly; the game swaps models when the cat sits.

Phase 2 props (2026-11-13): the chest, buoy and jetty are Tripo H3.1 with detailed texture and standard geometry. `chest-open.glb` is the same chest opened in code by `open-chest.mjs`, with no new generation: the one-piece mesh is cut at the top of the iron band (0.31 m, so the band, the lock plate and the handles stay on the box), everything above swings back 105° about the rear edge of the cut, and the script builds what the closed model never had: the rim of the box (6 cm walls, so that nothing of the old shell shows inside), an inside 0.55 x 0.35 m and 0.24 m deep, and the underside of the lid. The new faces use one flat wood-coloured patch of the same texture and are shaded board by board with vertex colours (`COLOR_0`), so it is still one material and one texture; it has 2,446 triangles. Each is a list of parts as in `sfx_crew.py`, with three more keys: `glide` plays a part at a speed that slides from one value to another, `die` lets a part run out, and `ring` adds the part again through two low resonances of its own. `find-drag` is one bubble played two to three times slower, which makes a single low gulp about a second long with no splash in it; neither file shares a recording with `bite-dunk`. `haul-knock` is a wooden knock slowed and with everything above 2.2 kHz taken off, ringing on near 120 and 330 Hz for a fifth of a second, with one small bubble 0.1 s in: without the bubble both listeners take a slowed knock for a drum, and with it for a thud under water. Between 78 and 90 % of each knock's energy is under 400 Hz; the first file had three quarters of its energy under 200 Hz, where a laptop's speakers give little, until its lower resonance was held back (56 % now, and about a quarter in the other two). `haul-snag` is the same idea made heavier (the knock at less than half speed, the ring near 80 and 220 Hz) and then the strain: a wooden creak and a creaking tree together from 0.12 s, each played at a speed that climbs by half as it goes, so the pitch rises about seven semitones. `haul-free` is a footstep in sand played slower and dulled, which is a soft scrape, and two or three single bubbles after it, each quieter than the last. `find-surface` is a splash played a little slower with itself again an octave down under it for the weight, a second of running water that starts 0.2 s in and dies away, and three single drops from 0.95 s; a limiter keeps the splash's first spike from holding the whole sound down, so it sits at -17.5 dBFS beside `land-splash` at -17. Lengths: 1.05 and 0.95 s, 0.48 s, 0.90 s, 0.78 s, 1.58 s. `find-drag` sits at -19.7 dBFS, held there by the peak of its first file; the rest are at -17.

Checks of the first group, with 61 descriptions and 149 files: 70 files put their own description first and 115 put it in the top three; the 138 older files came out byte for byte as before (with the second group: 66 descriptions, 156 files, 70 first and 118 in the top three, and the 149 earlier files unchanged). All three knocks, both `find-surface` files and `find-drag_2` put their own description first, and `find-drag_1` third. `haul-snag` comes fifth in both files, behind `haul-knock` among others, and `haul-free` sixth and eighth: the tagger hears the bubbles in every one of these and little else, and has no word for a creak (it gives the game's older creaks no "Creak" tag either), so these two rest on what their parts read as alone: the snag's creak stretch as "a squeak" and "a nail squealing out of wood", the scrape as "a wave" and a whoosh. No person has heard any of them. What to listen for: whether the gulp is plainly not the bite, whether the knock carries over the reel on small speakers, whether the snag's creak is heard as the line tightening, and whether the scrape of `haul-free` is heard at all under its bubbles.

The other five. `crate-open` is a short high creak (the lid levered up, a nail squealing; the creaks of `chest-open` played at full speed or near it, where the chest slows them down), a crack of wood 0.3 s in as the lid gives, a wooden rattle from 0.56 s for what is inside shifting, and one quiet drip; there are no coins in it. `relic-found` is synthesised: one struck note of glass on A5 that rings for three seconds, and from 0.45 s a low hum 35 cents under D3, the bell's note and the fifth below the glass, which swells until 1.5 s (there it stands at -24 dBFS with the glass down to -32) and is gone with the glass by 3 s. The hum is two voices 0.7 Hz apart that wander a few cents, with a little second and third harmonic so that something of it is left on small speakers. `rumour` is the page of `page-turn_1` with its flick 0.2 s in, and from 0.3 s one soft far note of glass on E6, 3 dB under the page; the whole is levelled 6 dB under the other sounds (-23 dBFS). `pearl-set` is a recorded tick and, 12 ms after it, a clear note of glass with a short tail (A6 in the first file, B6 in the second); `pearl-lift` is the A6 again, shorter, sinking three semitones in 0.3 s. The pearl sounds are levelled 4 dB under the rest (-21 dBFS) and `relic-found` 3 dB under (-20): a clear note that rings on is heard as louder than a knock or a splash that measures the same. The notes (A5, E6, A6, B6) are in D major, with the sea-road chimes and the quest chime. Lengths: 0.95 s, 3.30 s, 1.02 s, 0.72 s, 0.45 s.

Checks of these five: both `crate-open` files and `relic-found_1` put their own description first, `pearl-lift_1` second, `pearl-set` sixth and eighth (behind the other glass sounds; its tags are Ding 0.8 and Clang 0.2) and `rumour_1` sixth (its page stretch reads as "a page turning" 0.71 and its note as "one soft high note" 0.99). The crate's tags are Crack 0.15 and 0.42, and its first stretch is a Squeak (0.41) in the first file. The tagger calls the stretch of `relic-found` where the hum comes in "Sine wave" (0.12), so the hum may sound more like a test tone than like something answering. No person has heard any of them. What to listen for: whether the crate's creak is too like the chest's; whether the low note of `relic-found` is heard at all on small speakers, and whether it is strange or only wrong; whether the pearl's ring is pleasant when several are set one after another; and whether `rumour` is quiet enough under the music.
